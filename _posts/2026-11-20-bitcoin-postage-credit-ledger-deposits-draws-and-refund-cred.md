A prepaid credit balance makes high-volume bitcoin postage simple. You make one deposit, pay one network fee, then draw labels from the balance without waiting on a blockchain payment each time. The trouble starts a few weeks later, when deposits, draws and refund credits blur together and nobody remembers which coin transfer covered which batch.

A small running-balance sheet fixes that. Your account at the [Bitcoin Shipping Labels site](https://www.bitcoinshippinglabels.com) already keeps label history, so the sheet's job is to mirror that history line for line and attach the crypto side.

## Columns for your own sheet

Name these however you like. None of it maps to any export format from us.

| Column | What goes in it |
|---|---|
| Date | When the entry hit the balance |
| Type | DEPOSIT, DRAW or REFUND |
| Reference | Tx hash for deposits, tracking number for draws and refunds |
| Coin / invoice amount | Deposits only: coin and exact crypto amount due |
| Credit in | Deposit or refund value credited |
| Credit out | Label cost |
| Balance | Previous balance + in − out |

Keep the crypto amount and the credited value in separate columns. They're in different units. Your wallet's network fee is paid on top and goes to the network, so it belongs in your crypto records instead of this ledger. Before sending a deposit, make sure the amount that arrives covers the full invoice. Some exchanges subtract their withdrawal fee from the amount you type, which can leave it short.

## Refund rows

Unused labels the carrier hasn't scanned can be refunded as account credits. Trusted Partners request these themselves in the account. Everyone else messages support with the tracking number. Log each one as its own REFUND row with that same tracking number, and leave the original DRAW in place. Record the credit actually received and compare it with the original draw. Filter for a tracking number to find its DRAW and REFUND rows, or sort by Reference to group matching entries.

## Monthly reconciliation

1. Review your account's label history against your DRAW rows. Sort your own sheet by date if that helps.
2. Confirm every REFUND has a matching DRAW above it.
3. Compare your last Balance cell to the balance shown in the account.

If the numbers disagree, look for missing or incorrect rows, refunds included. Folding bitcoin postage into wider store bookkeeping?
