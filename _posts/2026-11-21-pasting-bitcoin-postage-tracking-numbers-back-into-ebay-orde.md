Sellers who buy bitcoin postage outside eBay and use a manual workflow have to add tracking to each order themselves. With six parcels on the desk, it's easy to paste number three into order four.

The buyer then gets a tracking link for someone else's parcel. You can fix it, but it's annoying, and it muddies things if a dispute comes up later.

## Build a matching sheet keyed on the eBay order number

Labels from [bitcoinshippinglabels.com](https://www.bitcoinshippinglabels.com) are standard 4x6 PDFs that look like any other carrier label. The recipient name and address on each label already help you match it to an order. Names repeat, though, and two buyers in the same town can look alike at a glance. A spreadsheet keyed on the eBay order number gives you a second check:

| eBay order no. | Buyer name | Ship-to ZIP/postal | Carrier | Tracking no. | Checked |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Fill the first three columns from eBay before you create any labels. When each PDF downloads, rename the file with its eBay order number. Then paste the tracking number into that row.

The optional free account keeps your label history, which helps if you need to rebuild a row. Without an account, you can recover a label with the order ID or the email you used at checkout.

## Double-check before marking shipped

Run these steps before you mark any order shipped:

1. Open the label PDF and read the recipient name and ZIP.
2. Compare them with the row in your sheet.
3. Compare that row with the eBay order page.
4. Paste the tracking number into eBay. Read the last few digits back against the PDF.
5. Tick "Checked" only when all three sources agree.

Work one order at a time. Swaps happen when you paste in batches. Also make sure the carrier you pick in eBay matches the carrier on the label.

## Confirm movement

Once the parcels are out the door, look up each number on the [package tracking page](https://www.bitcoinshippinglabels.com/track). No account needed. An acceptance scan means the carrier has a parcel carrying that label.

No scan after drop-off? Look into it: check the receipt from the counter or pickup, and confirm the parcel actually left. A scan won't tell you whether the right label went on the right box, so keep the label-to-order check from your sheet as its own step. Note the first scan date in your sheet so it's ready when a buyer asks.
