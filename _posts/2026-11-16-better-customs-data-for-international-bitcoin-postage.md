International bitcoin postage needs customs data that matches the packed parcel. A paid label doesn’t resolve an unclear description, an unsupported value or a missing country of origin.

Keep that payment flow separate from merchandise valuation: a crypto invoice amount shouldn’t automatically populate a customs value field. The receiver may owe import taxes depending on the service.

Treat customs readiness as a fulfillment checkpoint. Maintain the data in your own product records, then map it to the purchasing interface after checking its documentation.

- **Validate descriptions.** Identify the physical item, its material and its purpose where needed. “Woven polyester camera neck strap” communicates more than “Accessories.” Flag blanks, SKU-only text and marketing names for review.
- **Reconcile values.** Confirm whether the receiving form expects a unit value or a line total, and keep currency explicit. Use the required valuation basis and retain supporting records. Gifts, samples and replacements don’t automatically have zero customs value.
- **Count packed goods.** Use actual fulfillment quantities, not order or carton counts. Review bundles, multipacks, partial shipments and substitutions. Preserve separate product groups when their contents differ.
- **Verify origin.** Use supported production or manufacturing information under the applicable rules. Don’t copy the warehouse country by default. Sourcing changes may require batch-specific records.
- **Route exceptions.** Send uncertain valuation, origin or restricted-goods questions to a person before purchasing the label.
