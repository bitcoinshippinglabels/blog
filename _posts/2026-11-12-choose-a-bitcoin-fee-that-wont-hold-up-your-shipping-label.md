Before sending a btc postage payment, check current Bitcoin fee estimates. Your wallet’s default can be stale, and a shipping deadline leaves less room for a transaction to wait.

Treat checkout as two separate requirements: send the exact invoice amount and choose a competitive network fee.

## Pre-broadcast checklist

- **Check the payment window.** Leave time to review and broadcast. For an urgent label, start with the fee dashboard’s higher-priority estimate; a lower-priority rate accepts more waiting risk.
- **Refresh network estimates.** Read recommended rates, projected blocks and recent blocks on the mempool.space dashboard. Its view is an estimate, not a reservation in the next block.
- **Check units.** **sat/vB** is a rate, not the total fee. Network fee ≈ fee rate × transaction virtual size. Inputs, outputs and address types affect size.
- **Preserve the recipient amount.** Disable any “subtract fee from amount” option that would short-pay the invoice. An exchange can also deduct withdrawal charges from the amount sent.
- **Inspect the final preview.** Verify the destination, recipient amount, fee rate and total fee, then broadcast promptly. Demand can change while the transaction sits unsigned.

A competitive rate still can’t guarantee confirmation by a particular minute. Bitcoin blocks arrive at irregular intervals.

If payment looks stuck, distinguish wallet submission, network broadcast and processor detection. Start with the transaction ID. Check the recipient output, confirmation status and fee rate before considering your wallet’s replace-by-fee option. Any replacement must preserve the full payment to the same recipient. Don’t send a second independent payment just because the first is slow.
