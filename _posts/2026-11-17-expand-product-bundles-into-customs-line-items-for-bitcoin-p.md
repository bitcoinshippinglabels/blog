A storefront bundle is a sales unit; customs declarations describe the goods crossing a border. Before buying bitcoin postage, expand each kit SKU into its physical components, quantities, declared values, and countries of origin. Keep that transformation in your shop script or spreadsheet so every order follows the same rules.

## Define a component map

Maintain a versioned bundle table keyed by the storefront SKU. Each component record should carry:

- Component SKU and a specific goods description
- Quantity per bundle and unit of measure
- Component value, currency, and allocation basis
- Country of origin supported by supplier records

For a stationery kit, “paper notebook” and “ballpoint pen” describe the contents better than “starter bundle.” Multiply each component quantity by the ordered bundle quantity. Preserve the bundle reference internally for reconciliation, while keeping customs descriptions focused on the physical goods.

## Reconcile values and origins

Allocate the bundle’s sale value across components using a documented method consistent with applicable customs valuation rules. A proportional allocation based on standalone prices may be suitable, but check destination requirements. Handle discounts consistently and check rounding so component totals reconcile to the intended declared goods value.

Do not infer origin from your warehouse address or the supplier’s shipping location. Mixed-origin kits need component-level origin records. Flag missing descriptions, unsupported origins, zero values, and quantity mismatches for review before preparing bitcoin postage.

## Separate preparation from checkout

Your dashboard can show the expanded customs records beside the packed parcel’s weight and dimensions. Treat this as your shop’s validation layer, separate from label checkout.
