If you record shipping as a fiat expense, btc postage adds a question to every label: what was that bitcoin worth when it left your wallet? Paying the same invoice in USDC shrinks the question, since the coin is designed to track the dollar.

[Bitcoin Shipping Labels](https://www.bitcoinshippinglabels.com) accepts 100+ cryptocurrencies through NOWPayments, including BTC and USDC. The label is the same 4x6 PDF either way. Only the paper trail behind it changes.

## Fields to save per label

These are columns for your own spreadsheet.

| Field | BTC payment | USDC payment |
|---|---|---|
| Order ID | yes | yes |
| Invoice amount at checkout | yes | yes |
| Crypto amount sent | BTC, full 8 decimals | USDC amount |
| Transaction hash + chain | yes | yes |
| Network fee | separate line | separate line |
| Fiat rate at send time + source | yes | yes |
| Carrier + tracking number | yes | yes |

With btc postage, the rate row does most of the work. Pick one price source and keep using it. In many places spending bitcoin also counts as disposing of it, so cost basis may matter too. Log the rate for USDC as well. Stablecoins can drift off their peg, and recording the rate takes seconds.

## Two things that break reconciliation

- **Exchange withdrawal fees.** Some exchanges subtract their fee from the amount you type. The invoice arrives short and the label doesn't release. Check that the *received* amount covers the full invoice, and log the exchange fee on its own line.
- **Wallet network fees.** These are paid on top and go to the network, not to Bitcoin Shipping Labels. Keep them separate from the label cost.
