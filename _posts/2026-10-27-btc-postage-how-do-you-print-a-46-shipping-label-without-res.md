Buying btc postage and printing a usable label are two separate tasks. Treat the purchased PDF as an output artifact: inspect it, choose the correct print settings, and check the physical result before attaching it to a parcel.

Bitcoin Shipping Labels supplies labels as **4×6 PDFs**. That gives you a concrete target for the print workflow. The question is how to preserve that format when your PDF viewer, printer driver, and loaded paper each have their own settings.

This walkthrough focuses on that handoff—from downloaded file to attached label—with a repeatable preflight checklist for a single package or a small packing station.

## 2. Save the original PDF before changing settings

Once you have the label PDF, save it somewhere you can identify later. Use an internal order reference in the filename rather than putting a recipient’s address into it.

For example, adopt a naming convention such as:

`order-4821-label.pdf`

That example is a suggested local filename, not a required format.

Open the saved file in a PDF viewer and inspect it before opening the print dialog:

- Match the recipient and shipment to your packing record.
- Check that the expected carrier appears on the label.
- Inspect every page included in the file.
- Look at the page dimensions in the viewer’s document properties.
- Confirm that text and barcode areas appear intact.

Keep the original PDF unchanged. Make adjustments in print settings instead of rebuilding the label in an image editor or word processor.

**Separate the artifact from its presentation:** the saved PDF is your source; printer settings control how you put that source onto paper.

## 3. Choose your paper path

Decide which physical setup you’re using before adjusting scale.

### A dedicated 4×6 label printer

Load the intended label stock and select that printer explicitly. Check its configured paper size against the 4×6 PDF.

Look for these settings in the viewer or driver:

- Paper size: 4×6 inches.
- Scale: actual size or 100%.
- Pages per sheet: one.
- Orientation: matched to the preview.

Setting names vary, so use the preview to inspect the result rather than relying on a particular button label.

### A printer using a larger sheet

Keep the label at its original dimensions even when the loaded sheet is larger. Select the actual paper size for the printer, then preserve the PDF’s 100% scale.

Aim for a 4×6 label area placed on the sheet with clear space around it. Avoid enlarging the label to fill the entire page.

Before printing, check that the preview shows the whole label inside the printable area. Plan your trimming and attachment around the label boundary, keeping all printed content intact.

## 4. Resolve scaling before sending the job

“Fit” is a convenient setting for ordinary documents. For this workflow, use **actual size or 100%** as your starting point.

Review the preview in this order:

1. **Destination:** Is the intended printer selected?
2. **Stock:** Does the configured paper match what you loaded?
3. **Scale:** Is the PDF staying at its original size?
4. **Orientation:** Is the label positioned correctly?
5. **Bounds:** Are all edges and printed elements visible?
6. **Copies:** Are you sending the intended number?

If the preview looks wrong, change one setting at a time. Start with paper size, then orientation, then positioning. Keep scale fixed while investigating those settings.

Print one label as a test. Measure the printed label area against the 4×6 target, particularly when using a larger sheet. Inspect the barcode areas and small text under good light.

A screenshot is a poor troubleshooting artifact here. Return to the saved PDF and its print settings whenever you need to repeat the test.

## 5. Debug the physical result by symptom

A small troubleshooting table can keep repeated print attempts organized:

| Symptom | First check | Next test |
|---|---|---|
| Label appears too small | Scale and pages-per-sheet settings | Print at 100%, one page per sheet |
| Content runs beyond the stock | Selected paper size and orientation | Align those settings with the loaded stock |
| Label is sideways | Rotation and orientation controls | Change orientation and inspect the preview |
| Text or barcode areas look faint | Printer maintenance and print-quality settings | Follow the printer manufacturer’s instructions, then test again |
| An edge is missing | Preview bounds and paper configuration | Correct the configuration before another print |

Keep a short troubleshooting note with the printer name, paper size, scale, and result. A record such as “4×6 stock, 100%, portrait, right edge clipped” gives you a specific problem to investigate.

When only the print is defective, return to the saved PDF for the next attempt. Keep the purchase workflow separate from printer troubleshooting.

## 6. Match the finished label to the parcel

Move one parcel through the final check at a time. Place its packing record beside the printed label and compare the recipient details before attachment.

Inspect the physical label for:

- Complete text and barcode areas.
- Clear printing without missing sections.
- An attachment position away from folds and package edges.
- A surface where the label can lie flat.

For international shipments, provide the required customs information during label preparation. Inspect all pages supplied with the shipment and follow the applicable document-handling instructions before closing out the packing task.

Remove unsuccessful test prints from the packing area. Keep the original PDF associated with the correct shipment record, and record that the final print has been attached.

## 7. Save a reusable print preflight

Put this checklist near the printer or in your internal packing documentation:

- [ ] Correct shipment PDF opened.
- [ ] Original file saved under an internal reference.
- [ ] Intended printer selected.
- [ ] Loaded stock and configured paper size matched.
- [ ] Actual size or 100% selected.
- [ ] One page per sheet selected.
- [ ] Entire label visible in preview.
- [ ] First physical print inspected.
- [ ] Recipient matched to the parcel.
- [ ] Required shipment documents reviewed.
- [ ] Failed test prints cleared away.

Once a setup produces the intended result, record its settings for the next session. Repeat the physical test whenever you change the printer, stock, viewer, or driver configuration. That gives your packing process a clear acceptance check: the right PDF, printed at the intended size, attached to the right parcel.
