If you buy bitcoin postage for the same four or five box sizes every week, you can usually skip the tape measure and pull the numbers from a reference sheet. List each box's outer dimensions and its tare weight, meaning the box plus its typical void fill, tape and packing slip, with no product inside. That speeds up the label form and catches the classic mistake of entering only the product weight.

The label form at [www.bitcoinshippinglabels.com](https://www.bitcoinshippinglabels.com) asks for the packed weight and box dimensions before it shows carrier services and prices. Carriers charge by weight and size, so a wrong number on either one means the rate you're comparing doesn't match the parcel you're actually sending.

## What goes in the catalog

Add one row per box you actually stock. A spreadsheet works. So does a plain Markdown file in your repo or a printout taped above the scale.

| ID | Outer L × W × H (in) | Tare (oz) | Usual contents |
|----|----------------------|-----------|----------------|
| B1 | 8 × 6 × 4            | weigh it  | single mug     |
| B2 | 12 × 9 × 4           | weigh it  | two mugs       |
| B3 | 14 × 10 × 6          | weigh it  | bowl set       |

A few rules keep the numbers accurate:

- **Measure outer dimensions** on an assembled, taped box. Supplier listings can be off once the flaps are folded.
- **Weigh tare with the usual packing materials** in place: box, fill, tape and slip.
- **Remeasure when the packed shape changes.** An overstuffed box that bulges on top no longer matches its catalog row.
- **Re-weigh after supply changes** like a new supplier, thicker walls or a switch from paper fill to air pillows.
- **Add USPS Flat Rate boxes** if you use them. They can be selected for USPS and still need a weight entered.

## The sanity check

Before you type anything into the form, do one subtraction: scale reading minus tare should land close to the item's known weight. If the scale shows almost exactly the item weight, someone probably hit the tare button with the box on it, or the product got weighed before packing. Difference way bigger than expected? Look for a second item or extra fill.

That takes a few seconds and keeps you from buying a label for the wrong weight.
